LW channel tables (lw_*.dat):
Extracted from the EUVST sensitivity budget RSC-2022035 (the spreadsheet
of 29 May 2026, sheets LW1/LW2/LW3; NAOJ), current-best-estimate (CBE)
columns, on 2026-09-02. Wavelength converted from A to nm. Second-order
rows are indexed by the true photon wavelength. The spreadsheet also
carries CSR and Worst columns. The microroughness factor is NOT in these
tables because ECLIPSE computes it from microroughness_sigma with the
Debye-Waller factor: sigma = 0.6 nm reproduces the CBE column and 1.0 nm
the Worst column.

The budget's CBE effective area is reproduced by
  0.5 * geometric area * coating * Debye-Waller * grating * contamination * QE
to a constant 0.05 per cent on every row, the residual being the budget's
rounding of the geometric area (615.44 cm2 against 615.75 cm2 for 28 cm).
For example it gives 0.243 cm2 at H I 1215.67 A and 6.82 cm2 at H I 1025.72 A.

The detector efficiency drops from 0.297 at 1208 A to 0.010 from 1212 A to
the end of the LW3 table at 1224 A. That is the LW3 microchannel plate being
left without a photocathode from about 1210 A (SSD-SPC-ET004 Table 2 items
1-10 and 1-11: CsI on LW1 and LW3, KBr on LW2, "LW3 MCP bare for >~1210 A",
and a required 1-3 per cent at 1215.67 A), so that Lyman-alpha can be
observed without swamping the detector. Second-order LW3 light lands where
first-order light of twice its wavelength does, so second order meets the
bare region from about 605 A, and the column drops there too, from 0.372 at
605 A to 0.093 at 609 A. It drops less than in first order because a bare
MCP detects shorter wavelengths better.

This step is separate from Detector_LW.attenuation, the thin metal layer on
the small end of the fibre-optic taper that passes 1 per cent of the
phosphor light at every wavelength (RPR-SC-210013 section 3.2.2). Both apply.

The tables stop short of the detector's edges in places: LW1 at 845 A,
LW2 at 1039 A, and the second orders at 520 A and 609 A. A wavelength that
falls on the detector beyond them has no known throughput, and ECLIPSE
refuses it rather than extrapolate.
